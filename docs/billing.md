# Plans and payment

How an instance sells Spine, and how an operator sets it up. **Off by default**: with `SPINE_BILLING` unset nothing here applies, and an account is limited only by the instance's own ceilings (`SPINE_VAULT_MAX_MB`, `SPINE_SHARE_MAX_LIVE`, `SPINE_RELAY_DAILY_CALLS`). A self-hosted or private instance (`SPINE_INSTANCE=private`, which refuses `SPINE_BILLING=on`) leaves it off. The reasons for each design choice are in SECURITY.md **M-45**.

## The plans

| Plan | Price (Paddle's) | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Free trial | nothing, for three months from sign-up | Everything, with totals for the whole trial that are shown from the first day: 100 pages of AI reading (an archive import is part of them), 50 questions and 30 translated pages; 5 GB, share links, records for up to six people, and letting other accounts into them. At a total, or when the trial ends, the archive stays visible and exportable and only adding, asking and translating stop: the offer is Founding member or a plan. Only its pages read have a hidden ceiling (twice its 100); questions and translated pages have none, the totals stop them first |
| Free | nothing | One person's records, up to 50 pages kept, storage up to 1 GB, the timeline, search, export, the emergency card, one live share link without translation (opened 20 times and sending 250 MB a month), 3 pages of AI reading a month, kept for up to three months if unused (9 at most) |
| Essential | a yearly and a monthly price | One person's records, with as many documents as wanted, 2 GB, 5 pages of AI reading a month, kept for up to three months if unused (15 at most), questions about the records as often as needed within fair use, connections and related documents, share links (without translation) with no limit but the abuse ceiling, approval mode, and, with a yearly plan, a one-off allowance of 150 pages for reading an archive. No translation of documents. Sold only where both its prices are named |
| Premium | a yearly and a monthly price | One person's records, 5 GB, 30 pages of AI reading a month, kept for up to three months if unused, questions about the records and translation of the documents (a share link's too) as often as needed within fair use, connections and related documents, share links with no limit but the abuse ceiling, approval mode, and, with a yearly plan, a one-off allowance of 250 pages for reading an archive |
| Founding member | a yearly price | The yearly Premium for the first 500 accounts that ever take it, kept for as long as the subscription runs. A place is taken for good: it is not given back when the subscription is cancelled or the account is deleted (it is counted once, by the day, with no account in the count) |
| Family | a yearly and a monthly price | Premium for up to six people, with child profiles, other accounts let in, teenagers' own accounts and promotion at eighteen; up to six accounts can hold a seat in the plan (the one that pays and five invited), and these are the plan's, for them all: the six people (each account's own included), 25 GB, and twice Premium's pages of reading each month (questions and translation are not limited, as on Premium), with each account held to Premium's pages, and, with a yearly plan, a one-off allowance of 500 pages for reading an archive (250 for one account). Sold only where both its prices are named |

`core/src/billing/plans.ts` is the one place these are written: the relay, the routes and the account page all ask it, and the pricing page shows what the server tells it. **No amount is written in the code**: the server reads each price the operator named from Paddle (`GET /prices/:id`, `server/src/billing/catalog.ts`) and shows what it says, in its currency, kept ten minutes and kept longer if Paddle is out; with no answer the screens show no amount and offer no purchase. The checkout charges that same price, so the page and the payment cannot disagree. Make the prices tax-inclusive in Paddle (the plan sells at prices with VAT included), all in one currency, each plan with one yearly and one monthly price (a price that is not is refused and none is shown). Nothing is ever deleted when a plan ends: the records stay and export; what changes is what an account may add and use. Essential is sold only when `SPINE_PADDLE_PRICE_ESSENTIAL_YEAR` and `SPINE_PADDLE_PRICE_ESSENTIAL_MONTH` are set. The family plan is sold only when `SPINE_PADDLE_PRICE_FAMILY_YEAR` and `SPINE_PADDLE_PRICE_FAMILY_MONTH` are set. Changing from Premium to the family plan, or back, is done in Paddle's customer portal (a subscription to a price of the other plan is the other plan: the webhook reads it from the prices), not by a second checkout.

**What is sold** is the catalog on the admin page (`/admin/products`, `packages/billing/src/products.ts`): the application declares its products in `server/src/plans/products.ts` (which there are, and the periods and currency each is priced in), and an administrator edits the name, whether a product is available to buy and each amount. A price is never changed: a new amount is a new version, and **Send to the payment provider** makes it at Stripe (a product under an id of its own, a price under a lookup key, amounts tax included) and takes the version it replaces off sale there, which the subscriptions that bought it go on with. **Check against the payment provider** says where Stripe differs from the catalog. What a plan includes is never edited there: it is `core/src/billing/plans.ts`. A product the catalog holds prices for and has taken off sale is not sold, whatever the environment still names; Premium and the founding price cannot be taken off sale, since Spine cannot do without them. Prices named in the environment are taken into the catalog by one button (Import), which makes nothing at the provider and puts what it takes in on sale; where the catalog has a price that was sent, its id is the one the pricing page shows and a checkout charges. Paddle, the merchant of record, has its prices made in its own dashboard and the page does not change them. The Stripe key needs read and write on Products and Prices.

**The payment provider is behind one interface** (`packages/billing/src/provider.ts`): a checkout, the page where the customer manages the subscription, the price of a price id and the reading of a signed delivery, which each provider's adapter turns into one neutral event (a subscription changed, a payment completed, or nothing that matters). `packages/billing/src/events.ts` applies those events to an account whoever sent them, so the rules for who a message is for, whether it is news and what plan it leaves are written once. Paddle's adapter is `packages/billing/src/providers/paddle.ts`. One provider is active at a time (`SPINE_BILLING_PROVIDER`); an account's own row says who bills it, so a subscription made under one provider keeps working if another is chosen later. A new adapter passes the same contract test (`server/src/__tests__/billing-provider.test.ts`).

**Stripe** (`SPINE_BILLING_PROVIDER=stripe`, `packages/billing/src/providers/stripe.ts`) makes the operator the seller: Stripe takes the payment and nothing more, so VAT and the invoices are the operator's (a merchant of record, as Paddle is, does both). Its checkout is Stripe's own page, so there is no checkout page of ours and no address to name. In Stripe: make a price for each of the three plans' prices and for the ones the operator sells (a recurring one for each yearly and monthly, and one that does not repeat for the page top-up), in one currency, and name them in `SPINE_STRIPE_PRICE_*`; create a webhook endpoint at `https://<your domain>/api/billing/webhook`, pinned to API version `2026-10-28.endive` (the one the adapter asks for), for `checkout.session.completed`, `invoice.paid` and `charge.refunded` (a renewal, and a refund; only read when the operator issues invoices), `customer.subscription.created`, `customer.subscription.updated` and `customer.subscription.deleted`, and put its secret in `STRIPE_WEBHOOK_SECRET`; make a restricted key (Checkout Sessions: write; Customer portal: write; Prices: read; Invoices: read, for the invoices of renewals; Customers: write, where invoices are issued, to keep the billing details and read them for the invoice) for `STRIPE_SECRET_KEY`; and set the customer portal (its settings, not Spine's request, decide what a customer can do there) to: changing the payment method on, cancelling on and **at the end of the period** (not at once), **switching plans off** (a downgrade leaves a credit that Stripe applies at the next renewal, and that renewal's invoice then carries a negative line the invoices do not handle: a customer cancels and subscribes again instead), the invoice history off (Stripe's own invoices are not the fiscal ones; the plan card lists those) and updating the customer's details off (the billing details are asked for in the app). The sandbox has this as the default configuration `Spine: card and cancel only`; the live account needs the same. In Stripe's settings also turn on the failed-payment retries and emails, and the reminder before a yearly renewal. A subscription ended with `customer.subscription.deleted` ends the plan at once; one told to end at the period's end keeps it to the end. **One VAT rate for every buyer**: the invoices carry the one `SPINE_INVOICING_VAT_PERCENT`, which fits an operator that charges none, or one that charges Romania's to everyone (a seller under the EU's yearly threshold for services to consumers in other countries); a seller past it owes each buyer's country's rate and registers for the one-stop shop, and the invoices then need a rate for each country, which this does not have.

**Seats.** One subscription is one account's, the organizer's. The organizer invites other accounts from the account page (`POST /api/family/invites`); each link works once, for a week, and whoever opens it first while signed in takes the seat, so it is sent to the person only. A member has the plan's features, its people (six in all) and its allowance, which is counted under the account that pays (`server/src/plans/seat.ts`, `server/src/plans/gate.ts`). The subscription stays one price at quantity one in Paddle: a seat is not bought, and a household that needs more than six accounts takes a second family plan or, later, a larger plan, which is another entry in `PLANS`. When the organizer's plan ends, members have their own plan again and keep their seat for if it returns; nothing is deleted. `GET /api/family` and the routes under `/api/family/` answer 404 where there are no plans.

An account's plan is worked out, never stored: a running subscription gives the plan of its price (Essential, Premium or Family); otherwise the trial counts from the day the account was made; otherwise Free (`entitlementsOf`). A subscription set to end on a date keeps its plan to the end of what was paid for, and one cancelled at once (a withdrawal, a refund) ends it at once; a late payment keeps the plan a few days (`PAST_DUE_GRACE_DAYS`) while the provider tries again.

## Mail about using Spine

Three mails, from `server/src/lifecycle.ts`, with **no medical content** (no document, value, question or name; only counts the relay keeps) and written in the account's language: a **welcome** with the first step (add the documents), sent once when the address is confirmed; a **reminder** to add an appointment's documents, every so many days (a setting on the admin page, else `SPINE_NUDGE_DAYS`) from the day of the account or the last one (none unless set); and, with plans on, **two weeks before a trial ends**, once, what the account did (pages read, questions asked, pages translated) and the plan that fits (translation: Premium; questions: Essential; otherwise the free plan). The reminder and the trial mail carry a link to `/unsubscribe#…`, a page with one button, that stops those two for the account (a signed token only this server can make, in the fragment so no log has it; the welcome and the mail about the account itself are not stopped). The hourly jobs send them; each is claimed in the database, so any number of instances may run it. What was sent is a counter under the account (`mail:*` in the usage counters), never a copy. The server cannot see whether an import is finished, so no "import not finished" mail and no monthly summary are sent.

## The invitation bonus

An account's page has a link (`/register?ref=invite&by=<token>`); the token is the account's id and a signature only the server can make (`server/src/plans/invitations.ts`). When someone makes an account with it, or takes a seat in a family plan the account pays for, **both** get 10 pages, once for that person, and an inviting account is rewarded for at most five. The pages are those of a top-up: used after the month's, and not lost with the month. **Nothing keeps who invited whom**: the server checks the signature, adds the pages and counts on the inviter that one more was rewarded and on the invitee that they had theirs. The pages are given when the account is made, not when its first import is read, because that would need the server to remember the pair; a made-up account can still earn at most 50 pages for its inviter.

## The top-up

One thing is bought once, not by subscription: a **page top-up** (Essential, Premium and Family), 250 more pages for adding, by a Paddle price that does not repeat (`SPINE_PADDLE_PRICE_TOPUP_PAGES`). It is offered only in the app, on the account page, when the month's pages are nearly used (`server/src/plans/addons.ts`). The pages are used once the month's pages and what has carried are used up, and they do not run out with the month; the invitation bonus adds pages the same way. The purchase is applied once, by its transaction's id, however many times Paddle tells of it, from the `transaction.completed` message, by the price on it and never by what its custom data says, and counted on the buyer's own account. There is no translation pack: a person who wants translation for a trip takes Premium for a month, which costs less.

## What is measured

AI processing is measured at the relay, which sees every request. **Only adding is limited**: the pages read (an exact count, kept apart from everything else: a page of a scan is one, a document's text is as many as it has pages, said by the device and otherwise guessed from its size) stop at the plan's allowance, and while it lasts an archive import draws from its own one-off allowance; the hidden ceiling on pages read is twice what the account may add this month (the month's allowance, what has carried and the import of a yearly plan, so the import fits under it; the trial's is twice its 100), `server/src/plans/gate.ts`; the one-off import is for a **yearly** plan only (a bonus for paying a year, and it makes no sense for a plan whose pages come by the month): a yearly plan has all of it at once and a monthly plan has none (`importGrant`, `server/src/plans/carry.ts`); the smaller calls around reading (a document's start and end, stamps, summaries, links, embeddings) are not pages and are not counted as pages. **Using is not**: once a document is added it can be asked about and translated, for the owner or a doctor abroad through a link, as often as needed. Questions (a question is one message, however many calls the assistant makes) and translated pages (a document's pages once per language) are counted and shown on the account page and have no allowance on a plan; the trial is the exception, with a total of 50 questions and 30 translated pages for the three months, shown from the first day and refused once used (`trial:messages`, `trial:translated`). Behind every count a cost ceiling for each kind of work (`taskCost`, thousandths of a page: a fixed part for the task and a part for each kilobyte of the request) is a hidden limit, set far above any use of an archive and refused only past it, which bounds what an app that does not tell the truth, or a loop, can spend. A call is checked before it runs (the plan includes the task, the ceiling has room, and for a page the allowance has pages left) and charged after it succeeds, so a failed call costs nothing; at the limit of pages the call is refused (402, `allowance_used`) and the account page says the pages are almost used up from 80%. **Unused pages carry over** on Free and every paid plan: each month adds its allowance (3 on Free, 5 on Essential, 30 on Premium, 60 for a household's pool, each account's own part 30) to what the months before left, and at most three months' worth is kept (9, 15, 90, 180 for the pool, 90 for an account's part), so a person on Premium who adds nothing for three months can add 90 pages in the fourth and then starts again from what is left. A month is followed back twelve months, from the month the plan began to count (`bank:since`), and the trial carries nothing (its pages are one total for the three months). Several calls in flight at once can overshoot by a few; the relay's daily count bounds that. On a plan with seats a call is counted twice, in the plan's pool (under the account that pays) and in the account's own meter, and refused when either is used up: the pool is what the plan has, and the account's own meter, held to Premium's pages, keeps one account from adding all of them. **The weights are first guesses**; replace them with figures from the provider's usage logs. A share's recipient is held to the owner's plan for features (translation, questions); the recipient's translation is counted in the owner's translation count and anything else the recipient does is counted only against the share's own daily calls. Storage is a limit on adding too, and on a plan with seats it is for all its accounts together: what an account may add is what the plan has left after the others (`vaultBytesFor`), never above the instance's own ceiling for one account (`SPINE_VAULT_MAX_MB`, default 5120, so set it to at least the family plan's 25 GB where that plan is sold). How many share links are live is the plan's limit only on the free plan; elsewhere it is the instance's own ceiling for one account (`SPINE_SHARE_MAX_LIVE`). **How work is counted once:** the device gives every call of one question, and every call of one document's translation into one language, the same random id (`x-spine-work`), and says how many pages a call reads or translates (`x-spine-pages`); the first call of an id counts and the others do not. **Share links** are limited only on the free plan (one live link, opened 20 times and sending 1 GB a month): on a paid plan a link is for whoever needs to read it, a doctor abroad included, so how many are live, how many times they are opened and how many bytes they send are counted for the account page and refused only past a hidden fair-use ceiling no use reaches (below). Each fetch of a link's sealed copy is a view, its bytes and the bytes the owner's own devices read from the vault count in one pool, past a limit the link refuses its readers until the next month, an emergency card is never counted or stopped, and the owner's own reads are counted and never refused (`server/src/plans/links.ts`). **What an account has done in all** (the pages read for it, the questions answered, the pages translated) is kept as three numbers that only grow (`total:pages`, `total:messages`, `total:translated`: the account's own and never a pool's, with no month in the key so nothing forgets them) and shown on the plan card as things done, never against a limit. **Hidden fair-use ceilings** bound what one hostile account can cost; they are never shown (not in a response, a screen, an error or the pricing page, and not in what a browser is sent) and are the operator's configuration, not code: a JSON file (`SPINE_FAIR_USE_FILE`, default `fair-use.json` in the data directory) sets them for each plan and is read again within half a minute, so a change needs no release. There is a monthly ceiling, the burst guard, for questions, translated pages, link opens and link traffic, and a yearly one for questions, translated pages and link traffic (link opens are monthly only), over twelve months: for an annual subscription its billing year, for a monthly one or an operator's grant the last twelve months. On a plan with seats they are the household's, counted in the pool, and Family's are twice Premium's. Past one the action is refused, that one only, with the words "This account has reached its fair-use limit, contact support" (a link says only that it is not available just now), and nothing is made worse quietly: the model is the same at every volume. The pricing page does not say "unlimited": it says there is no monthly limit for normal use, and that automated or abusive use may be slowed or paused. **What the operator sees:** an estimate of what each account's AI use costs (the tokens sent and returned, at the prices in the file) is kept by the month and by kind of work, and an account that passes about 15 euros in a month, or 80% of any ceiling, is reported once for its month or year in the log and by mail to `SPINE_ALERT_EMAIL`; `npm run cli -- usage [--month=YYYY-MM] [--top=N]` lists the accounts by cost with their questions, translated pages, link opens and traffic, storage and how near each is to a ceiling, and the averages the plans' prices are to be checked against (tokens per question, translated pages per account, bytes per link open). **Documents** (the free plan's fifty) are counted by the app and not by the server, which cannot see into sealed records: the page tells the core the limit and the core refuses what would pass it (`core/src/plan-limits.ts`), before a file is kept and, once its pages are counted, before any is read; nothing is deleted. The limit counts pages, not documents, so grouping them differently changes nothing. A changed app could keep more, and still could not read, store or send more than the plan's meters and limits, which the server counts. **The import allowance** is once for the life of the account, not by the month: 250 pages (a pool of 500 for a family, each account held to 250), drawn by the reading of uploads the person marks as an archive import on the Upload page, while it lasts, and from the month's reading after.

The vault's size and the number of live shares are limited by plan in the routes that already enforced the instance's ceilings (`plans/entitlements.ts`). Approval mode is refused to Free on creating a link and on changing one.

## What the operator sees

Only counts, never a person (`server/src/dashboard.ts`). The page `/admin` (not the app: no menu, no records, not linked from anywhere, kept out of robots.txt; it signs in like everything else and comes back to itself) shows them to an address in `SPINE_ADMIN_EMAILS`, or in `SPINE_MARKETING_EMAILS` (the marketing role, which can read and change nothing), and, for an administrator, has pages of its own in a menu across the top: the overview (these numbers), **Subscriptions** (`GET /api/admin/subscriptions`: each account with a subscription, its plan and interval, who bills it, its state and what it is paid until; it names accounts, so the marketing role does not see it) with the withdrawal requests, **Invoices** with the e-Factura link, **Accounts** (those waiting for approval) and **Settings** with the hidden ceilings. The numbers (`GET /api/admin/dashboard`, worked out at most once a minute, one walk of the accounts at a time): sign-ups, checkouts, payments and link and card opens today, over 7 and over 30 days; where sign-ups came from (a link ending in `?ref=reddit`, `hn`, `producthunt`, `linkedin`, `diaspora`, `youtube`, `partner`, `share`, `card`, `invite` or `doctors`; any other word is not counted); the plans accounts are on and the founding places taken; how far accounts got (read pages, imported, asked, translated) overall and by week of sign-up; the estimated AI cost of the month by plan; and where people met a limit or a ceiling (`limit_pages`, `limit_messages`, `limit_translated`, `limit_import`, `limit_feature_*`, `fair_use`, counted when the relay refuses). Each guardrail turns amber below three quarters of its target and red otherwise. The server cannot see documents, the emergency card or an invitation, so "imported" means five or more pages read, and what only the app could report (upgrade prompts shown and clicked) is not here: the app sends no counters.

**Settings on the admin page** (`server/src/operator-settings.ts`, kept in the database's `operator_settings`, so they work on a host with no disk and change with no release): the dashboard's four targets (sign-ups in the last 30 days, the share that imported, the share that asked, founding places taken), the monthly AI budget, the AI cost at which one account is reported, and the days between reminder mails. Each shows where its value comes from: set on the page, else the variable (`SPINE_AI_BUDGET_EUR`, `SPINE_NUDGE_DAYS`) or, for the account alert, the fair-use file's `watch.aiEurosPerMonth`, else the default in the code (the targets: 300, 40%, 25%, 25). An administrator changes them, within each one's bounds; the marketing role only sees them; "Reset" goes back to the variable or the default. **The hidden ceilings are changed there too**, for an administrator only (never the marketing role, and never sent to anyone else), in a folded section "Hidden ceilings": every ceiling of every plan, the share of a ceiling at which you are told, and the two prices of a million tokens the cost estimate uses (`server/src/plans/fair-use-admin.ts`). They are kept in the database as one setting, `fair_use`, in the same shape as the fair-use file, so they work on a host with no disk (Cloud Run) and are the same for every instance; the order is the built-in value, then the file, then the page, and "Reset" removes the page's value. Every instance reads them again within half a minute. Each change needs the password to have been typed in the last ten minutes, is checked against a bound (a ceiling up to 10^13, a multiple up to 100, a share from 0.05 to 1) and is logged without its value's owner. Everything else stays in the environment: secrets, Paddle's prices, the model, the mailer, who may sign up and who is an administrator.

The monthly AI budget: the hourly jobs add up the estimated cost of every account and the operator is told at half, four fifths and all of it, once each, in the log and by mail to `SPINE_ALERT_EMAIL`. The figures are the relay's estimates by the token, a warning in time and not an invoice.

## Setting it up with Paddle

Paddle is the merchant of record: it charges, issues invoices and accounts for VAT, and Spine never sees a card.

1. In Paddle, create three prices on one product: Premium's yearly and monthly prices and the founding yearly price, and, to sell Essential or the family plan, two more for each (a yearly and a monthly price), and the 250-page top-up (a price that does not repeat). The amounts are yours to choose there and are shown as Paddle has them; none is set in Spine. Set each price's quantity to a minimum and maximum of 1: Paddle allows up to 100 by default and its checkout then offers a quantity picker, so a person could pay for several and still get one plan. Create a notification destination for `https://<your domain>/api/billing/webhook` with the events `subscription.created`, `subscription.activated`, `subscription.updated`, `subscription.canceled`, `subscription.past_due`, `subscription.paused`, `subscription.resumed`, `subscription.trialing` and `transaction.completed`, and copy its secret. Create an API key and a client-side token.
2. Serve `checkout/` from **its own origin** (for example `pay.<your domain>`), copy `checkout/config.example.js` there under the name config.js and fill it in, and make that address Paddle's default payment link (Checkout, Checkout settings, Default payment link). A checkout opens there: Spine does not name the page in its request, because Paddle refuses an address whose domain it has not approved, in the sandbox too, and a default that is another page is logged. It must not be the app's origin: Paddle's script then never runs where a person's records are open. Its content security policy needs Paddle's script, frames and connections (see Paddle's guidance) and nothing of the app's.
3. Set the variables below on the server and restart. `PADDLE_ENVIRONMENT` has no default because one value charges real money: try everything in `sandbox` first.

| Variable | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| `SPINE_BILLING` | `on` to have plans. Off by default; refused with `SPINE_INSTANCE=private` |
| `PADDLE_ENVIRONMENT` | `sandbox` or `production` |
| `PADDLE_API_KEY`, `PADDLE_WEBHOOK_SECRET` | The API key (creates a checkout, opens the customer portal) and the notification destination's secret (checks a message is Paddle's) |
| `SPINE_PADDLE_PRICE_YEAR`, `SPINE_PADDLE_PRICE_MONTH`, `SPINE_PADDLE_PRICE_FOUNDING` | The ids of the three prices |
| `SPINE_PADDLE_PRICE_ESSENTIAL_YEAR`, `SPINE_PADDLE_PRICE_ESSENTIAL_MONTH` | Essential's two prices; both or neither |
| `SPINE_PADDLE_PRICE_FAMILY_YEAR`, `SPINE_PADDLE_PRICE_FAMILY_MONTH` | The family plan's two prices; both or neither |
| `SPINE_PADDLE_PRICE_TOPUP_PAGES` | The 250-page top-up, a price that does not repeat; unset: not sold |
| `SPINE_CHECKOUT_URL` | The address of the checkout page above, the same as Paddle's default payment link (a checkout that opens elsewhere is logged) |
| `SPINE_WAITLIST` | `on` shows "Tell me when Spine opens" on the front page while accounts cannot be made |

The relay applies the plans and needs only `SPINE_BILLING=on` and the store (it never calls the payment provider, so it does not need its settings), and it shares the server's database, so with several instances use Postgres (`SPINE_STORE=postgres`).

## Invoices when the provider is not the seller

Paddle is the seller of record and sends the invoice. Stripe is not: the operator issues it, and in Romania sends it to ANAF's e-Factura, through an invoicing service, or with Spine's own link to ANAF (`SPINE_INVOICING=efactura`: docs/efactura.md, which is the same queue with another issuer). `packages/billing/src/invoicing/issuer.ts` is the interface (`InvoiceIssuer`: `issue` once for a payment, `sendToAnaf`, and `repeatSafe`, whether the service documents that a repeated request for the same payment returns the first invoice), and the service is one adapter behind it, `billyou.ts` (`billyouIssuer`): changing service is writing another adapter, and nothing else names Billyou. **How a payment becomes an invoice** (`SPINE_INVOICING`, off by default, and only with Stripe): a payment event (a checkout session, or a renewal's invoice from Stripe) carries the provider's reference for the payment (`saleRef`); the webhook puts it on a queue (`invoice_queue`: the reference, where the invoice has got to, the service's series and number; no name, address or amount) and tries it at once, and the hourly job (`processDueInvoices`) tries again what did not finish. Each try reads the sale from Stripe again (`readSale`: when, the currency, the buyer as checkout collected it, the lines at what was paid), so nothing of the buyer is kept here between the payment and the invoice. A refused invoice is left for the operator (`failed`: a log line and a count a day, `invoice_failed`); one that may pass is tried ten times over about a day. A service that cannot tell a repeated request from a new one (`repeatSafe` false) is not asked again after an attempt that ended with no answer: the invoice may exist, and the row says `check`. The VAT is the operator's setting (`SPINE_INVOICING_VAT_PERCENT`), taken out of what the buyer paid, and an invoice in another currency than RON takes the National Bank's rate (`bnr.ts`). The invoice is recorded as paid at the service (Billyou: a payment with the provider's reference), since the buyer paid before it existed. **Billing details.** Stripe's checkout asks a Romanian buyer for a city and a postal code and not a county, and e-Factura needs a real one (Billyou refuses a city), so where the operator issues the invoices the app asks for the billing details before a purchase (`BillingDetailsForm`, `core/src/billing/billing-details.ts`): a name, the country, the city and street, and in Romania a county (the code ANAF takes), and a sector in Bucharest. They are saved as the customer at Stripe (`saveBuyer`: the county in the address's state, the tax id and sector in its metadata), the table `billing_customers` keeps only the customer's id, the checkout is made for that customer and does not ask for an address, and the invoice's buyer is read from the customer (`readSale`), so it is what the app collected whatever the payment page took. A purchase made without them is answered 409 `billing_details_required`, and the app opens the form. A payment made before the details existed falls back to the page's address, and a Romanian one without a county is left `failed` with that reason. The customer stays at Stripe when the account is deleted (the payments are Stripe's records); the id here goes with the account. **A refund reverses the invoice**: Stripe's `charge.refunded` is traced from the charge's payment intent to what was queued for the payment (the checkout session, or the renewal's invoice, reached through the invoice payments), and a storno row (`storno:` and that reference) is queued and tried like an invoice, then sent to e-Factura if the operator asked. A refund of part of a payment cannot be reversed this way (a storno is the whole invoice) and one of a payment whose invoice is not made cannot either: both are left as `check` with the reason, for the operator. **The right of withdrawal.** Where the operator is the seller (Stripe), the payment page makes the buyer tick a box before paying (`consent_collection`, with `withdrawalConsent` as its words, in the buyer's language, linking the terms and the refund policy): the service starts now, the right of withdrawal ends once it is fully provided, and a service used before a withdrawal is paid for as used. Stripe keeps the answer with the session; Stripe's own account settings need the terms address (`/terms`) for the box to link to it.

**A buyer withdraws, and the operator decides.** Where the operator is the seller (Stripe) and the account has a subscription that runs, the plan card offers "Withdraw from this purchase" for fourteen days from the first payment (`POST /api/billing/withdraw`; where the first payment's day is not known, because invoices are not issued, it is offered and the operator judges the time). The request is one row (`withdrawal_requests`: the account, the day, the decision, what was paid back; one open per account), the buyer is mailed the confirmation of it (a durable medium, in the account's language) and the operator is told. On `/admin/subscriptions` the operator sees the open requests, each with what the account used since its first payment (pages read, questions and pages translated, from the counters; `usedSince` in `usage-report.ts`) and how many earlier requests were paid back, so a purchase made to use the service for a few days and then taken back shows as that. It also shows a **suggested amount to pay back** (`packages/billing/src/withdrawal-share.ts`, the last payment read from Stripe by `lastPaid`): the whole payment where nothing was used, otherwise the payment less the share of its period that has run (a day begun counts), as the refund policy says; a button pays it back, and the operator may enter another amount. The operator then either **pays back and ends the plan** (all of the last payment, or an amount: `withdraw` ends the subscription first, with nothing billed for the rest of the period, and then refunds the last payment, each made once for the request, so a refund that fails leaves the request open to be tried again) or **declines with a reason**; the buyer is told which. The refund's own event then has the invoice reversed (a part refunded is left for the operator as `check`). The key needs Refunds: write and Subscriptions: write. A top-up bought once has no subscription to end: it is refunded in Stripe's dashboard and the invoice is still reversed. **Invoice history**: the account's invoices (and credit notes) are listed on the plan card with a Download for each, whose link is asked of the invoicing service when pressed, since it does not last.

**The operator's tools.** An invoice that is refused, or tried ten times, or whose attempt ended with no answer at a service that cannot tell a repeat (`check`), is told to the operator by mail (`SPINE_ALERT_EMAIL`: the reference and the reason, never a buyer) and shown on `/admin` (Invoices: how many are in each state, the ones that stopped with the service's own words, "Try again", which puts one back where it had got to, and "Mark as done by hand"; only an administrator, with a recently typed password) and by `npm run cli -- invoices [retry|settle <reference>]`. The connection to ANAF ending (`SPV_TOKEN_UNAUTHORIZED`) is not a failure: such an invoice waits and is tried again, so renewing the connection in Billyou lets it through. **e-Factura is asynchronous at Billyou**: a submission returns "processing" and ANAF decides within seconds to hours, so the invoice stays queued (`issued`) and is asked about every five minutes, for up to a day (then `check`), until ANAF validates it (done) or rejects it (`failed`, with ANAF's reason and the fix, to be corrected in Billyou and tried again); the submission is read before it is made, since a second one for an invoice ANAF holds is refused, and an ANAF fault is submitted again by itself. **The EU threshold**: each invoice records what it counts for in the yearly limit on sales to consumers in other EU countries (a person with no tax id, in a member state other than Romania, in EUR at the National Bank's rate; a storno takes its original's off), and the hourly job tells the operator once at 70%, at 90% and when it is passed (`watchEuThreshold`); `/admin` and the CLI show the year so far.

VAT is left to the accountant (the adapter takes the rate per line).

## Checking it

`npm test -w spine-server` covers the signature check, the order of messages, the founding places, the gate and the limits. With Paddle's sandbox: buy with a test card from the account page, watch `subscription.created` arrive, confirm the account page says Premium, cancel from the portal, and confirm it says the subscription ends and Premium stays to the end of the period. A webhook that cannot be placed (a subscription message ahead of its purchase) is answered 409 and Paddle sends it again.

## Operating it

- `npm run cli -- grant-plan you@example.com --days=90 [--plan=essential|premium|family]` gives the paid plan without a payment (a friend, a reviewer); `revoke-plan` ends it.
- `npm run cli -- waitlist` lists the addresses that asked to be told when Spine opens, `waitlist-remove` removes one.
- `npm run cli -- metrics --days=30` shows the counts the marketing plan measures: `signup`, `signup_from_share`, `signup_from_doctors`, `signup_from_card`, `signup_from_invite`, `share_opened`, `card_opened`, `checkout_started`, `checkout_started_page_topup`, `paid_event`, `addon_page_topup`, `invitation_bonus`, `founding_taken`, `second_subscription` (an account that had a plan was given another subscription, so one of the two may be billing for nothing: it is logged too, and the operator decides which to refund and cancel), `limit_pages`, `limit_messages`, `limit_translated`, `limit_import`, `limit_feature_*`, `fair_use` and the `budget_alert_*` steps. They are numbers a day under a name, never who.
- An account with a running subscription cannot be deleted until the subscription is cancelled, so a person who leaves is not charged afterwards.
