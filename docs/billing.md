# Plans and payment

How an instance sells Spine, and how an operator sets it up. **Off by default**: with `SPINE_BILLING` unset nothing here applies, and an account is limited only by the instance's own ceilings (`SPINE_VAULT_MAX_MB`, `SPINE_SHARE_MAX_LIVE`, `SPINE_RELAY_DAILY_CALLS`). A self-hosted or private instance (`SPINE_INSTANCE=private`, which refuses `SPINE_BILLING=on`) leaves it off. The reasons for each design choice are in SECURITY.md **M-45**.

## The plans

| Plan | Price | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Free trial | €0 for six months from sign-up | Everything, with 150 pages of AI reading in all, questions and translation, 5 GB, share links, records for up to six people, and letting other accounts into them |
| Free | €0 | One person's records, up to 50 documents, storage up to 1 GB, the timeline, search, export, the emergency card, one live share link without translation (opened 20 times and sending 1 GB a month), 10 pages of AI reading a month |
| Premium | €39 a year or €3.99 a month | One person's records, 5 GB, 30 pages of AI reading a month, kept for up to three months if unused, questions about the records and translation of the documents (a share link's too) as often as needed within fair use, connections and related documents, share links with no limit but the abuse ceiling, approval mode, and a one-off allowance of 250 pages for reading an archive |
| Founding member | €29 a year | The yearly Premium for the first 500 paying accounts, kept for as long as the subscription runs |
| Family | €79 a year or €7.99 a month | Premium for up to six people, with child profiles, other accounts let in, teenagers' own accounts and promotion at eighteen; up to six accounts can hold a seat in the plan (the one that pays and five invited), and these are the plan's, for them all: the six people (each account's own included), 25 GB, and twice Premium's pages of reading each month (questions and translation are not limited, as on Premium), with each account held to Premium's pages, and a one-off allowance of 500 pages for reading an archive (250 for one account). Sold only where both its prices are named |

`core/src/billing/plans.ts` is the one place these are written: the relay, the routes and the account page all ask it, and the pricing page shows what the server tells it. The amounts must match the prices created at the payment provider. Nothing is ever deleted when a plan ends: the records stay and export; what changes is what an account may add and use. The family plan is sold only when `SPINE_PADDLE_PRICE_FAMILY_YEAR` and `SPINE_PADDLE_PRICE_FAMILY_MONTH` are set. Changing from Premium to the family plan, or back, is done in Paddle's customer portal (a subscription to a price of the other plan is the other plan: the webhook reads it from the prices), not by a second checkout.

**Seats.** One subscription is one account's, the organizer's. The organizer invites other accounts from the account page (`POST /api/family/invites`); each link works once, for a week, and whoever opens it first while signed in takes the seat, so it is sent to the person only. A member has the plan's features, its people (six in all) and its allowance, which is counted under the account that pays (`server/src/billing/seat.ts`, `server/src/billing/gate.ts`). The subscription stays one price at quantity one in Paddle: a seat is not bought, and a household that needs more than six accounts takes a second family plan or, later, a larger plan, which is another entry in `PLANS`. When the organizer's plan ends, members have their own plan again and keep their seat for if it returns; nothing is deleted. `GET /api/family` and the routes under `/api/family/` answer 404 where there are no plans.

An account's plan is worked out, never stored: a running subscription gives Premium; otherwise the trial counts from the day the account was made; otherwise Free (`entitlementsOf`). A cancelled subscription keeps Premium to the end of what was paid for, and a late payment keeps it a few days (`PAST_DUE_GRACE_DAYS`) while the provider tries again.

## What is measured

AI processing is measured at the relay, which sees every request. **Only adding is limited**: the pages read (an exact count, kept apart from everything else: a page of a scan is one, a document's text is as many as it has pages, said by the device and otherwise guessed from its size) stop at the plan's allowance, and while it lasts an archive import draws from its own one-off allowance; the smaller calls around reading (a document's start and end, stamps, summaries, links, embeddings) are not pages and are not counted as pages. **Using is not**: once a document is added it can be asked about and translated, for the owner or a doctor abroad through a link, as often as needed. Questions (a question is one message, however many calls the assistant makes) and translated pages (a document's pages once per language) are counted and shown on the account page and have no allowance. Behind every count a cost ceiling for each kind of work (`taskCost`, thousandths of a page: a fixed part for the task and a part for each kilobyte of the request) is a hidden limit, set far above any use of an archive and refused only past it, which bounds what an app that does not tell the truth, or a loop, can spend. A call is checked before it runs (the plan includes the task, the ceiling has room, and for a page the allowance has pages left) and charged after it succeeds, so a failed call costs nothing; at the limit of pages the call is refused (402, `allowance_used`) and the account page says the pages are almost used up from 80%. **Unused pages carry over** on Premium and Family: each month adds its allowance (30 on Premium, 60 for a household's pool, each account's own part 30) to what the months before left, and at most three months' worth is kept (90, 180 for the pool, 90 for an account's part), so a person who adds nothing for three months can add 90 pages in the fourth and then starts again from what is left. A month is followed back twelve months, from the month the paid plan began to count (`bank:since`), and Free and the trial carry nothing. Several calls in flight at once can overshoot by a few; the relay's daily count bounds that. On a plan with seats a call is counted twice, in the plan's pool (under the account that pays) and in the account's own meter, and refused when either is used up: the pool is what the plan has, and the account's own meter, held to Premium's pages, keeps one account from adding all of them. **The weights are first guesses**; replace them with figures from the provider's usage logs. A share's recipient is held to the owner's plan for features (translation, questions); the recipient's translation is counted in the owner's translation count and anything else the recipient does is counted only against the share's own daily calls. Storage is a limit on adding too, and on a plan with seats it is for all its accounts together: what an account may add is what the plan has left after the others (`vaultBytesFor`), never above the instance's own ceiling for one account (`SPINE_VAULT_MAX_MB`, default 5120, so set it to at least the family plan's 25 GB where that plan is sold). How many share links are live is the plan's limit only on the free plan; elsewhere it is the instance's own ceiling for one account (`SPINE_SHARE_MAX_LIVE`). **How work is counted once:** the device gives every call of one question, and every call of one document's translation into one language, the same random id (`x-spine-work`), and says how many pages a call reads or translates (`x-spine-pages`); the first call of an id counts and the others do not. **Share links** are limited only on the free plan (one live link, opened 20 times and sending 1 GB a month): on a paid plan a link is for whoever needs to read it, a doctor abroad included, so how many are live, how many times they are opened and how many bytes they send are counted for the account page and refused only past a hidden fair-use ceiling no use reaches (below). Each fetch of a link's sealed copy is a view, its bytes and the bytes the owner's own devices read from the vault count in one pool, past a limit the link refuses its readers until the next month, an emergency card is never counted or stopped, and the owner's own reads are counted and never refused (`server/src/billing/links.ts`). **What an account has done in all** (the pages read for it, the questions answered, the pages translated) is kept as three numbers that only grow (`total:pages`, `total:messages`, `total:translated`: the account's own and never a pool's, with no month in the key so nothing forgets them) and shown on the plan card as things done, never against a limit. **Hidden fair-use ceilings** bound what one hostile account can cost; they are never shown (not in a response, a screen, an error or the pricing page, and not in what a browser is sent) and are the operator's configuration, not code: a JSON file (`SPINE_FAIR_USE_FILE`, default `fair-use.json` in the data directory) sets them for each plan and is read again within half a minute, so a change needs no release. There is a monthly ceiling, the burst guard, for questions, translated pages, link opens and link traffic, and a yearly one for questions, translated pages and link traffic (link opens are monthly only), over twelve months: for an annual subscription its billing year, for a monthly one or an operator's grant the last twelve months. On a plan with seats they are the household's, counted in the pool, and Family's are twice Premium's. Past one the action is refused, that one only, with the words "This account has reached its fair-use limit, contact support" (a link says only that it is not available just now), and nothing is made worse quietly: the model is the same at every volume. The pricing page does not say "unlimited": it says there is no monthly limit for normal use, and that automated or abusive use may be slowed or paused. **What the operator sees:** an estimate of what each account's AI use costs (the tokens sent and returned, at the prices in the file) is kept by the month and by kind of work, and an account that passes about 15 euros in a month, or 80% of any ceiling, is reported once for its month or year in the log and by mail to `SPINE_ALERT_EMAIL`; `npm run cli -- usage [--month=YYYY-MM] [--top=N]` lists the accounts by cost with their questions, translated pages, link opens and traffic, storage and how near each is to a ceiling, and the averages the plans' prices are to be checked against (tokens per question, translated pages per account, bytes per link open). **Documents** (the free plan's fifty) are counted by the app and not by the server, which cannot see into sealed records: the page tells the core the limit and the core refuses what would pass it (`core/src/plan-limits.ts`), before a file is kept and before documents are made; nothing is deleted. A changed app could keep more, and still could not read, store or send more than the plan's meters and limits, which the server counts. **The import allowance** is once for the life of the account, not by the month: 250 pages (a pool of 500 for a family, each account held to 250), drawn by the reading of uploads the person marks as an archive import on the Upload page, while it lasts, and from the month's reading after.

The vault's size and the number of live shares are limited by plan in the routes that already enforced the instance's ceilings (`billing/entitlements.ts`). Approval mode is refused to Free on creating a link and on changing one.

## Setting it up with Paddle

Paddle is the merchant of record: it charges, issues invoices and accounts for VAT, and Spine never sees a card.

1. In Paddle, create three prices on one product: yearly (€39), monthly (€3.99) and the founding yearly price (€29), and, to sell the family plan, two more: yearly (€79) and monthly (€7.99). Set each price's quantity to a minimum and maximum of 1: Paddle allows up to 100 by default and its checkout then offers a quantity picker, so a person could pay for several and still get one plan. Create a notification destination for `https://<your domain>/api/billing/webhook` with the events `subscription.created`, `subscription.activated`, `subscription.updated`, `subscription.canceled`, `subscription.past_due`, `subscription.paused`, `subscription.resumed`, `subscription.trialing` and `transaction.completed`, and copy its secret. Create an API key and a client-side token.
2. Serve `checkout/` from **its own origin** (for example `pay.<your domain>`), copy `checkout/config.example.js` there under the name config.js and fill it in, and make that address Paddle's default payment link (Checkout, Checkout settings, Default payment link). A checkout opens there: Spine does not name the page in its request, because Paddle refuses an address whose domain it has not approved, in the sandbox too, and a default that is another page is logged. It must not be the app's origin: Paddle's script then never runs where a person's records are open. Its content security policy needs Paddle's script, frames and connections (see Paddle's guidance) and nothing of the app's.
3. Set the variables below on the server and restart. `PADDLE_ENVIRONMENT` has no default because one value charges real money: try everything in `sandbox` first.

| Variable | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| `SPINE_BILLING` | `on` to have plans. Off by default; refused with `SPINE_INSTANCE=private` |
| `PADDLE_ENVIRONMENT` | `sandbox` or `production` |
| `PADDLE_API_KEY`, `PADDLE_WEBHOOK_SECRET` | The API key (creates a checkout, opens the customer portal) and the notification destination's secret (checks a message is Paddle's) |
| `SPINE_PADDLE_PRICE_YEAR`, `SPINE_PADDLE_PRICE_MONTH`, `SPINE_PADDLE_PRICE_FOUNDING` | The ids of the three prices |
| `SPINE_PADDLE_PRICE_FAMILY_YEAR`, `SPINE_PADDLE_PRICE_FAMILY_MONTH` | The family plan's two prices; both or neither |
| `SPINE_CHECKOUT_URL` | The address of the checkout page above, the same as Paddle's default payment link (a checkout that opens elsewhere is logged) |
| `SPINE_WAITLIST` | `on` shows "Tell me when Spine opens" on the front page while accounts cannot be made |

The relay applies the plans and needs only `SPINE_BILLING=on` and the store (it never calls the payment provider, so it does not need its settings), and it shares the server's database, so with several instances use Postgres (`SPINE_STORE=postgres`).

## The routes

| Route | Who | What |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `GET /api/pricing` | anyone | The prices, whether the family plan is sold, and the founding places left; `{ enabled: false }` where there are no plans |
| `GET /api/billing` | signed in | The account's plan, what it has used (AI reading, storage, share links), what it includes, whether it can manage billing, and whether it pays for a plan others hold a seat in or holds a seat in another's (`seat`); a seat's plan and allowance are the organizer's |
| `POST /api/billing/checkout` | signed in | Starts a purchase for `{ interval, plan }` at the provider and answers where to send the person; refused for an account that already has a plan |
| `POST /api/billing/portal` | signed in, with a Paddle subscription | Opens the provider's page for the card, invoices, switching and cancelling |
| `GET /api/family` | signed in | Whose plan this is: for the organizer the seats, the accounts that hold them, the invitations waiting and how many people the plan holds; for a member the organizer and the same count |
| `POST /api/family/invites`, `DELETE /api/family/invites/:id` | the organizer (a recently typed password to make one) | Makes an invitation (its secret is in the answer once) or takes one back |
| `DELETE /api/family/members/:id` | the organizer (a recently typed password) | Ends a member's seat; the member is mailed |
| `POST /api/family/preview`, `POST /api/family/join` | signed in, holding the secret | Who an invitation is from; takes the seat |
| `POST /api/family/leave` | a member | Gives the seat up |
| `POST /api/billing/webhook` | the provider only | Its signed messages; anything not signed correctly is refused |
| `POST /api/waitlist` | anyone | An address to be told when Spine opens; the same answer whether or not it was there; five a minute per source, 200 an hour from all sources together, and at most `SPINE_WAITLIST_MAX` (20,000) on the list |

## Checking it

`npm test -w spine-server` covers the signature check, the order of messages, the founding places, the gate and the limits. With Paddle's sandbox: buy with a test card from the account page, watch `subscription.created` arrive, confirm the account page says Premium, cancel from the portal, and confirm it says the subscription ends and Premium stays to the end of the period. A webhook that cannot be placed (a subscription message ahead of its purchase) is answered 409 and Paddle sends it again.

## Operating it

- `npm run cli -- grant-plan you@example.com --days=90 [--plan=family]` gives the paid plan without a payment (a friend, a reviewer); `revoke-plan` ends it.
- `npm run cli -- waitlist` lists the addresses that asked to be told when Spine opens, `waitlist-remove` removes one.
- `npm run cli -- metrics --days=30` shows the counts the marketing plan measures: `signup`, `signup_from_share`, `signup_from_doctors`, `signup_from_card`, `signup_from_invite`, `share_opened`, `card_opened`, `checkout_started`, `paid_event`. They are numbers a day under a name, never who.
- An account with a running subscription cannot be deleted until the subscription is cancelled, so a person who leaves is not charged afterwards.
